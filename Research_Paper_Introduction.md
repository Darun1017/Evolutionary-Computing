# 1. Introduction

The development of artificial intelligence (AI) for complex, competitive game environments remains a formidable challenge in computational research. Real-Time Strategy (RTS) games, in particular, provide exceptionally rich testbeds for AI methodologies due to their massive state-action spaces, imperfect information, and the requirement for simultaneous multi-unit control and long-term strategic planning. Traditionally, game AI relies heavily on handcrafted heuristics, predefined state-action rules, or extensive offline training designed for static environments. While these methods can achieve high momentary performance against specific adversaries, they fundamentally lack the capacity for continuous learning and struggle to generalize when faced with dynamic shifts in the game environment, such as varied map topographies, resource constraints, or evolving opponent behaviors. 

Genetic Algorithms (GAs) have emerged as a powerful paradigm for addressing these limitations. By mimicking the biological processes of selection, crossover, and mutation, GAs are highly effective at exploring expansive, multi-dimensional search spaces to continuously optimize competitive agent behaviors. However, conventional GAs face critical limitations when applied to highly dynamic strategy games. Standard GA approaches often evaluate an agent's fitness based on a single momentary objective—such as its immediate win rate in a specific generation. This reliance on momentary fitness frequently results in catastrophic forgetting, premature convergence upon local optima, and a loss of strategic diversity. Furthermore, traditional GAs rely on static, manually tuned mutation rates that do not adapt to the agent's current performance context, reducing their scalability in complex environments like RTS games.

To address these optimization issues, this paper proposes a novel, advanced **Genetic Algorithm** framework for the strategy optimization and co-evolution of competitive game agents. Rather than training agents once for a fixed goal or using static fitness evaluations, our proposed GA introduces a system where agents learn and improve continuously in response to shifts in the game environment itself. Specifically, we implement our enhanced GA through two trajectory-driven models: the **Trajectory-Driven Cellular Co-Evolutionary Algorithm (TD-CCEA)** and the **Trajectory-guided Self-adaptive Co-Evolutionary Algorithm (TSCEA)**. 

Unlike traditional GAs, our proposed algorithm evaluates agent chromosomes (strategies) using *weighted performance histories* across multiple generations. By calculating environmental centroids—representing the mathematical meta-state of the population at a given time—agents are scored based on their adaptive capability over time rather than a single generation's victory or defeat. This context-aware trajectory score directly drives a *self-adapting mutation rate*: agents with highly successful historical traits undergo fine-tuning (low mutation), while poorly performing agents mutate aggressively to escape strategic dead ends, removing the need for manual parameter tuning. 

To ensure the GA maintains a healthy, diverse gene pool and remains robust against changing conditions, we implement a two-layer diversity preservation mechanism that prunes both consistently weak individuals and redundant genetic copies. Evaluated within the **microRTS** simulation environment across a vast 14-dimensional strategy space (encompassing aggressive rushes, defensive holds, and economic expansions) and complex blockade maps, our findings demonstrate the profound efficacy of our trajectory-guided GA.

The primary contributions of this paper are:
1. The formulation of an advanced Genetic Algorithm (implemented via TD-CCEA and TSCEA) that utilizes trajectory memory and environmental similarity weighting to evaluate fitness over time.
2. The introduction of dynamic, self-adapting genetic mutation rates tied directly to historical trajectory scores, solving the traditional GA problem of fixed exploration-exploitation trade-offs.
3. An empirical validation of the proposed GA against standard baseline algorithms, demonstrating superior strategy optimization, avoidance of premature convergence, and robust long-term adaptability in high-dimensional strategy environments.
